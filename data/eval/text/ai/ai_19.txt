Great question! The sky is blue because of the way sunlight bounces around in the air.

Sunlight looks white, but it's really made of all colors mixed together. When it enters the atmosphere, it bumps into tiny gas molecules. Blue light travels in shorter, choppier waves, so it gets bounced around far more than other colors and scatters across the whole sky — that scattered blue light is what reaches your eyes from every direction.

At sunset, light has to travel through much more air, so most of the blue gets scattered away before it reaches you, leaving the reds and oranges behind — which is why sunsets glow warm.
